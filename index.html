Featured Work

Predicting Flight Delays with Machine Learning

Flight delays cost airlines money and damage customer trust. Predicting a delay before it happens gives airlines and passengers a chance to plan ahead instead of reacting after the fact.

539,383
flight records analyzed
64.5%
accuracy — best model (KNN)
4
models trained & compared
5
person team
01

The Data

539,383 real flight records — airline, route, day, time, and flight length — labeled as delayed or on-time. A near-balanced split (55.4% on-time / 44.6% delayed) made this a realistic classification problem, not an artificially clean one.

02

My Role

Part of a 5-person team building this system end-to-end.

My focus was the data side: exploring the 539K-record dataset, identifying which features carried real predictive signal (and which — like the raw id column — needed to be dropped before scaling), shaping the feature set, and training and evaluating the four models compared in this project.
03

Approach

Four classification algorithms were trained and compared head-to-head — Logistic Regression, KNN, SVM, and Naive Bayes — to find which one best captured the delay patterns in the data.

04

Results

KNN won on both accuracy and F1 — though the trade-off is real: no training phase, but it has to compare every new flight against the full training set, making it the slowest model at prediction time.

The Deliverable

The winning model was packaged into a working Tkinter desktop app — enter flight details, get an instant Delayed / On-Time prediction with probability. Not just a notebook result — a usable tool, end to end.
